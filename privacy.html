Support · Privacy

Mathonauts has no accounts and collects nothing about you or your child. Everything stays on the device, unless a grown-up chooses to move progress to another device — and even then it leaves encrypted, and we cannot read it.

  • No accounts. There is no sign-up, email address or password.
  • On the device. Explorer names, PINs, progress, outfits and settings are saved in the app's own storage on your device. PINs are stored only as a salted one-way fingerprint (SHA-256), never as the digits themselves.
  • No ads, analytics or tracking. There are no third-party SDKs, no advertising identifiers, and nothing measuring what your child does.
  • No purchases. There are no in-app purchases. Gems are earned by learning and cannot be bought.
  • Moving progress between devices — optional, and only a grown-up can start it. In the PIN-protected Parent Zone you can create a ten-character recovery code. The app encrypts the save on your device (AES-256-GCM, under a key stretched from the code with PBKDF2) and stores it with our hosting provider, Supabase, under a name that is a one-way hash of the code. The code itself is never sent. What is stored is a meaningless identifier and a block of ciphertext — we cannot read it, and neither can the host. Typing the same code into the other device brings the progress across. If you never open that screen, the app makes no internet connections at all.
  • What a backup holds. The same things the app keeps on the device: explorer names, progress, badges, outfits, settings and the PIN fingerprint. No email address, no phone number, no location, no device or advertising identifier.
  • Deleting data. Delete backup, on that same screen, erases the stored copy for good. Removing an explorer in the Parent Zone deletes that child's progress on the device. Deleting the app deletes everything it holds. A backup that nobody has saved to for a year is deleted automatically.
  • Children. The app is made for children, shows no advertising and collects nothing for profiling, in line with COPPA and GDPR-K. The only thing that ever leaves the device is a backup a parent has asked for.
